Unit 5: Advanced Chording

Chapter 1: Compound Chord

A compound chord isn't one chord — it's two or more separately trained chords, fired one after another quickly enough to land inside a shared "compound timeout" window. Chorded in sequence within that window, they combine into a single piece of output instead of staying separate.

The classic example: a chord for "know" followed, within the timeout, by a chord for "ledge" — together they output "knowledge", not "know ledge". Miss the timing window and you'd just get the two chords' outputs back to back instead.

This is what makes a modest set of trained chords go a long way: rather than training a separate chord for every long word, you can train a handful of common word-parts and compound them on the fly.
