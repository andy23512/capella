Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 2: Impulse Chord

Training a chord normally means opening your device's software (Device Manager) and assigning switches to an output ahead of time. An impulse chord skips that: it's a chord you create on the spot, right where you're typing — no separate app required.

It's called up by i+DUP, a hard-coded chord baked into the firmware that always triggers Impulse Chord and can't be changed. In Device Manager you can also assign Impulse Chord to another key or chord of your own if you'd like a different trigger. Either way, triggering Impulse Chord walks you through a short prompt right in your text: perform the chord input you want to assign, call up impulse, type the output it should produce, confirm the output, then confirm the captured input — pressing Escape at any point starts you over instead of leaving you to delete what you've typed.

What it's for: capturing a one-off chord the moment you notice you're about to retype the same phrase again, without breaking your flow to go train it properly first.

The flow

  1. 1

    Chord the input (optional)

    Perform the combo you want to assign, anywhere you can type. Jumbled letters mean the combo is free; if it prints an existing output, continuing will overwrite that chord. You can skip this and perform the combo at step 4 instead.

  2. arrow_downward
  3. 2

    Call impulse: i+DUP

    The prompt >I<mpulse output: appears. Anything you chorded before it may sit in front of the prompt — that is fine.

  4. arrow_downward
  5. 3

    Type the output, then press Enter

    Everything you type is recorded as the output, so use Escape rather than Backspace if you slip.

  6. arrow_downward
  7. 4

    Check the input, then press Enter

    The prompt >I<mpulse input: shows the captured combo. If it is not what you want, or is empty, perform the combo you want and it updates. Enter saves the chord to your device.

  • Escape at any step starts you over, so you never have to delete what you typed.
