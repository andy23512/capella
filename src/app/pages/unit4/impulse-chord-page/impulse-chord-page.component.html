Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 2: Impulse Chord

Training a chord normally means opening your device's software (Device Manager) and assigning switches to an output ahead of time. An impulse chord skips that: it's a chord you create on the spot, right where you're typing — no separate app required.

It's called up by a hard-coded chord baked into the firmware, not something you configure yourself: i+DUP. Performing it walks you through a short prompt right in your text: perform the chord input you want to assign, call up impulse, type the output it should produce, confirm the output, then confirm the captured input — pressing Escape at any point starts you over instead of leaving you to delete what you've typed.

What it's for: capturing a one-off chord the moment you notice you're about to retype the same phrase again, without breaking your flow to go train it properly first.
