Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 1: Simple Chord

Everything in Units 1–3 was character entry: one switch at a time, in sequence, the same way a regular keyboard works. A CharaChorder's other mode is chording — pressing and releasing several character switches at the same time to output something else entirely, like a whole word, instead of those characters one by one. A simple chord is the most basic form of this: a fixed set of switches, trained to one output.

A CharaChorder device actually ships with a set of "Starter Chords" already loaded, covering some of the most common words in English — but you can also train your own, add more via Device Manager, import a shared chord library, or create one on the fly with impulse chording. The diagram below shows one of those built-in starter chords: pressing B and C together outputs the word "because" instead of typing it letter by letter.

B + C = "because" — one of CharaChorder's built-in Starter Chords

Because character entry and chording share the same switches, you don't have to choose one or the other — CharaChorder devices default to "fluid" entry: press one switch at a time and it types normally, or press several switches down together and release them together — within the device's Press Tolerance and Release Tolerance windows — and it fires the trained chord instead.
