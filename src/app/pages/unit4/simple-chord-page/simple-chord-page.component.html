Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 1: Simple Chord

Everything in Units 1–3 was character entry: one switch at a time, in sequence, the same way a regular keyboard works. A CharaChorder's other mode is chording — pressing and releasing several character switches at the same time to output something else entirely, like a whole word, instead of those characters one by one. A simple chord is the most basic form of this: a fixed set of switches, trained to one output.

Chords aren't built in — there's no factory-default word list, they're trained by you (or synced from a shared chord library). So the diagram below isn't a real chord; it's purely illustrative: it shows the switches for D, O, and G lit up together, standing in for what it would look like if you trained that combination to output "dog" instead of typing it letter by letter.

Illustrative example — not a real trained chord

Because character entry and chording share the same switches, you don't have to choose one or the other — CharaChorder devices default to "fluid" entry: press one switch at a time and it types normally, or press several together within the chord timing window and it fires the trained chord instead.
