Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 3: Chord Modifier

A chord modifier is an input added alongside a chord that changes its output rather than replacing it — the chord still fires, just altered. There are five:

  • Capitalization — capitalizes the output ("run" → "Run")
  • Present Tense — ("work" → "working")
  • Plural — ("book" → "books")
  • Past Tense — ("help" → "helped")
  • Comparative — ("small" → "smaller")

Each of these five has a fixed switch — it's not something you choose yourself the way a chord's own switches are. On the devices this site covers (CC1, CC2, CCU, and Master Forge), that mapping is the same: Capitalization is either Shift switch, Present Tense is the left AT (Ambidextrous Throwover) switch, Plural is the right AT switch, Past Tense is the left Numeric Layer switch, and Comparative is the right Numeric Layer switch.

The AT switches do double duty depending on context — held by themselves they mirror the opposite hand's letters, the Ambidextrous Throwover feature from the last unit, but held together with a chord they act as the Present Tense/Plural modifier instead. The diagram below shows that second case: the left AT switch, tapped right after the "work" starter chord, turns its output into "working".

O + R + W, then left AT = "working" — the "work" starter chord with the Present Tense modifier

A modifier can technically be added at the same time as the chord, but that's a tight timing window in practice — arpeggiately, firing the chord first and tapping the modifier switch right after, is what actually works and what the exercises below use.
