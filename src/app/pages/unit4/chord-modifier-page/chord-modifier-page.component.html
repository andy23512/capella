Unit 4: Basic Chording

Chapter 3: Chord Modifier

A chord modifier is an input added alongside a chord that changes its output rather than replacing it — the chord still fires, just altered. Common changes include:

  • Adding a prefix or suffix ("run" → "rerun", "run" → "running")
  • Capitalization ("run" → "Run")
  • Conjugation or tense ("run" → "ran")
  • Part of speech ("run" the verb → "run" the noun's plural, "runs")
  • Language — switching a chord's output to another trained language
  • Structure — reshaping how the output is put together entirely

Like a simple chord, exactly which switches act as modifiers is something you train yourself — there's no fixed default. What is fixed is the timing option available to trigger one: a modifier can be added at the same time as the chord, or arpeggiately — a quick tap right after — which is the timing mechanic covered next.
