Chapter 2: 3D switch introduction

Every switch on a CharaChorder device is a 3D switch: instead of one on/off state, it senses which of four directions it's tilted in — north, east, south, or west. On CC1, CC2, and CCU, each switch also senses a straight-down press as a separate fifth state, making it a true 5-way switch that can stand in for up to five different inputs. Master Forge's switches are 4-way instead — see below for how their center press differs.

Orientation differs by device

The five directions above are the same on every device — but which way is physically "north" is not. On CC1, CC2, and CCU, the non-thumb switches are oriented to match natural finger movement, so tilting a switch feels like moving your finger in that direction. Master Forge changes this: its non-thumb switches are oriented to match the edge of the digitizer half instead, roughly a 45° twist away from where your finger naturally wants to go.

Exactly where "natural" points can vary a bit from person to person and hand to hand, so treat the photos above as a starting point rather than gospel — try tilting each switch yourself and confirm the direction that feels right for your own fingers.

"the orientation of the non-thumb switches aligns with the digitizer (half device)" — from Tangent's review of Master Forge, which covers the practical impact (and a layout-viewer workaround) in more depth.

One more Master Forge difference worth knowing: each of its switches is built from four unidirectional microswitches underneath, with no separate center sensor. So pressing straight down isn't sensed as its own state the way it is on CC1/CC2/CCU — it's read as all four cardinal directions firing at once.
